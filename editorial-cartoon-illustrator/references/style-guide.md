# Style Guide v1.3

## Priority order
1. **One clear visual contradiction** over narrative complexity.
2. **Compositional breathing room** over filling the frame.
3. **Caption integrated into one page** over typography added as a footer.
4. **Economical character drawing** over fine-grained facial and garment detail.
5. **Strict neutral monochrome** over warm, nostalgic toning.
6. **Original, relevant metaphor** over generic symbols or inherited object bans.

## Composition
- Overall image aspect ratio **4:3**, including the caption area.
- Inset the illustration with ample margins; suggested side space 6–10% and top space 5–8% are flexible visual guides, not cropping rules.
- Keep important people, props and structures away from the outer edge so that their roles remain legible.
- Normally use two clearly related focal groups: the apparent success and the telling counterpoint. A different arrangement is acceptable if the central contradiction stays immediately clear.
- Draw the caption on the same continuous paper tone below the illustration, separated by a natural pause. Avoid caption strips, hard boundaries and oversized blank bars.

## Visual style
- Neutral black ink, neutral gray wash, white or neutral ivory substrate; **no sepia, yellow, brown or warm cast**.
- Clean expressive contours and selective cross-hatching; do not hatch every surface.
- Slightly caricatured, simple and believable faces. Gesture and posture carry emotion; avoid photographic likeness and portrait-level modeling.
- Architecture, background figures and objects give context and may reward a second look, but must not crowd the essential action.
- Use 3–6 foreground characters as a soft target, not a quota. Include only the number required to tell the story clearly.

## Metaphor selection
- Extract a specific tension from the source before choosing the setting or props.
- Prefer recognizable, believable everyday situations containing one subtle but meaningful contradiction.
- Avoid generic, overused or irrelevant financial and technology symbols. Do not ban objects by name because one prior illustration used them poorly.
- An otherwise familiar object is welcome when it is native to the subject or carries an original narrative function.
- Change settings, props and visual rhythms across pieces. Maintain the aesthetic identity without repeating the same formula.

## Caption
- Exactly one short English caption below the illustration, horizontally centered, Caslon-like italic serif, in double quotation marks.
- Reproduce a user-supplied caption exactly; otherwise compose an original dry, intelligent line.
- The caption complements the contradiction instead of explaining it. No speech bubbles.
- Use natural spacing and matching ink/paper so the text belongs to the printed artwork.
- Aim for one line, but never shrink it to illegibility merely to force that format.

## Avoid
- Crowding, ornamental overload, repetitive gestures and background action without narrative value.
- Warm filters, sepia, colored wash, heavy cinematic effects, photographic textures, flat vector, logos and identifiable public figures.
- Edge-to-edge artwork, clipped subjects, detached captions and oversized blank bands.
- Literal financial infographics or stock-market shorthand used in place of a thoughtful scene.

## Feedback handling
- Long-term preference: carry forward explicitly.
- One-off image defect: fix locally, do not add a permanent forbidden-object rule.
- Repeated defect: translate into a general quality principle.
- One-topic requirement: keep scoped to that topic unless instructed otherwise.
- When user feedback compares images, treat the preferred image as a directional reference, not a template to copy exactly.

## Quality checks
- Is the central contradiction readable in 3–5 seconds?
- Is there a strong focal point and a clearly legible counterpoint?
- Could any person, prop or ornament be removed without weakening the idea? Simplify where useful.
- Is the composition open around the edges and is the caption visually part of the same page?
- Is the artwork entirely neutral grayscale?
- Did the chosen metaphor emerge from the source thesis, rather than from a generic icon or an old prohibition?
